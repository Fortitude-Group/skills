# Phase 3 — PRESSURE (Adversarial Pass)

Purpose: argue *against* the candidate properly, before enthusiasm and
build-excitement skew judgment. Mandatory before any endorsement. The
pass is only complete when every attack below has been made in writing
and either killed the candidate or been answered with evidence (not
optimism). "Partially survives" is a legitimate outcome — record what it
does to scope, pricing, or sequencing.

## The standard attacks (run all that apply)

1. **The date attack.** Search for delay/repeal/scale-back news dated in
   the last 90 days. Has the deadline moved before? Governments that
   delay once delay twice. Is there lobbying for exemption or a mandated
   free tier?
2. **The already-happened attack.** Did the first panic wave already
   occur? Who absorbed it? Customers acquired in a panic wave become
   annually-recurring customers *of the incumbent* with compounding
   switching costs.
3. **The free-competitor attack.** Is there a free official tool,
   guidance document, template, or "just use a Word doc" path that
   satisfies the median buyer? For compliance products this is the
   default incumbent. What, specifically and recurringly, does the paid
   product do that the free path doesn't sustain?
4. **The loved-incumbent attack.** Re-read the drill. If incumbents are
   5★-loved at the target price, what precisely is the entrant's claim?
   "Cheaper than beloved" and "slightly better UX" both fail.
5. **The information-advantage attack.** Is this opportunity being
   blogged by every professional adviser in the country? Universally
   known + low barriers = content war against entrenched SEO, which most
   solo operators lose. What does THIS operator know/have that the crowd
   doesn't?
6. **The feature-not-a-company attack.** Could an adjacent platform ship
   this as a feature next quarter? Which one, and what's their incentive?
   If the answer is "any of three, easily", the candidate needs either a
   speed thesis or a kill.
7. **The GTM-collision attack.** Does winning require a sales motion the
   scope file excludes (relationship sales, founder brand, outbound)?
   Route-to-buyer can veto here regardless of other scores.
8. **The treadmill attack.** Does the product depend on scraping,
   unofficial APIs, or fighting platform countermeasures forever? That's
   an operating cost masquerading as a moat — usually a kill.
9. **The revenue-cliff attack.** What does month-after-the-deadline
   revenue look like? One-off migration demand is a services business.
10. **The gatekept-input attack.** Does the core product depend on data,
    licences, or accreditation controlled by a competitor, platform, or
    authority? If yes, the candidate needs a **GATE ZERO**: a named desk-
    research checklist (pricing quotes, licence terms, portal checks)
    with a deadline, which must pass BEFORE any build effort — including
    before a validation site. Gate zero costs research time, not build
    budget, so it can run in parallel with other work.

## Steelman, then verdict

After the attacks, write the best *counter*-argument for each surviving
attack — with evidence, not hope. Then score the rubric and issue the
verdict (Phase 4 vocabulary) once Phase 3b has produced the power-path
score. If the pass killed the candidate, lead the
write-up with that, name the killing evidence, and cite its primary
source and date. Killing a previously-endorsed candidate is a process
victory; frame it that way explicitly.

## Instant-kill triggers

Every surviving candidate leaves this phase with a written list of
events that kill it immediately at any future time (deadline slips,
official free tool ships, incumbent locks the distribution channel,
platform closes the gap, licensing changes). These go in the dossier and
are re-checked at every later phase boundary and checkpoint.

## Next: Phase 3b

A candidate that survives this pass goes to Phase 3b
(`phase-3b-powers.md`) before any verdict is issued. Surviving the
attacks proves it can win customers; Phase 3b decides whether it can keep
them once a funded rival copies the code.
