# Phase 2 — DRILL

Purpose: for one candidate, establish exactly what the incumbents fail at
and whether the failure is one a new entrant can own. The drill either
finds a wedge or kills the candidate — "the market is big" is not a wedge.

## Step 1 — Re-verify the load-bearing facts
Before spending any drill effort, re-check the facts the candidate stands
on (Iron Rule 2): the deadline, the mandate's scope, the pricing cliff.
Search for delay/repeal/softening news dated *after* the mining pass.
If a load-bearing fact has moved, stop and re-verdict immediately.

## Step 2 — Incumbent complaint mining
Identify the top 3–6 incumbents (include non-software incumbents:
membership bodies, consultancies, the regulator's free tooling, "a
spreadsheet"). For each:
- Trustpilot/G2/Capterra/app-store reviews — read the 1–3★ tail AND the
  complaints buried inside 5★ reviews ("only downside is…").
- Forum/Reddit/community sentiment, vendor-response tone.
- Pricing page (primary source), free-tier existence, per-transaction vs
  subscription resentment.
- Momentum check: have they already shipped features addressing the
  candidate's thesis? (Incumbents responding fast to the same trigger is
  a leading kill indicator.)

## Step 3 — Classify every recurring complaint
- **STRUCTURAL** — the incumbent *cannot* fix it without breaking their
  business model, architecture, or price point (e.g. enterprise pricing
  that can't chase £20/mo buyers; a regulator-imposed constraint they
  monetise; a platform they can't integrate with). Structural complaints
  are the only valid wedges.
- **FIXABLE** — the incumbent can patch it in a sprint (missing feature,
  UX gripe, price they could cut). Fixable complaints are NOT wedges;
  list them only to show they were considered.
- **NOT ADDRESSABLE** — imposed on everyone (regulator taxonomy, platform
  ToS). No one can own these; they're moat-neutral.

## Step 4 — The three-way empty-lane check
If the thesis is "nobody serves segment X", prove it: three
differently-phrased competitor searches minimum (product-category terms,
buyer-language terms, "alternative to Y for Z" terms). Log the searches
in the dossier. Finding well-loved 5★ incumbents with review moats in the
target price tier is a kill, not an obstacle — switching costs compound
annually and love is a moat.

## Step 5 — Wedge statement or kill
End the drill with one of:
- **A wedge statement**, one sentence: "Incumbents structurally cannot X
  because Y; the entrant owns X by Z." This feeds Phase 3.
- **A kill**, with cause of death and salvage clause (e.g. "park the
  vertical as a distribution channel for existing product P"; "the B2B
  component angle survives as a watchlist item with an inbound-only
  trigger").

Record the drill's verbatim gold — the two or three quotes that best
prove the pain — in the dossier. Verbatims outlive arguments.
